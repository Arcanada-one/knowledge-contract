## Abstract

The paper proposes a model in which extracted content, the conditions for understanding it and the conditions of a new application are addressed separately. For each task a finite set of requirements is formed, and applicability is judged against knowledge editions, contexts, the addressee, the method and available grounds. Unknown conditions and conflicts are kept explicit. A deduplication boundary lets an identical content core be reused while source occurrences and differing conditions remain. The main edition gives definitions, conditional properties and a test plan and ran no three-book tests; the appendix keeps later limited runs, including a negative result.

## Claims and their basis

- Naive deduplication fails in illustrated cases: a record for 4 servings and one for 6 should not merge, and two books copying one source are not two independent witnesses. Basis type: authors-domain-assessment; examples are illustrative, not measured.
- Conditional properties CP01-CP08 separate logical consequences of definitions from empirical hypotheses; an unknown value does not pass a mandatory barrier. Basis type: authors-domain-assessment.
- Applicability improves for concrete tasks at a justified cost of explicit structure. Basis type: hypothesis; nothing beyond illustrated is claimed.
- The bounded executable profile reports 103 checks passed with no errors. Basis type: finite-test; declared operations and author examples only, groups not independent experiments.
- Appendix protocol for addressed revision: 30 cases from three books, two implementations per case, 60 executions matching expected outcomes. Basis type: finite-test over a finite area; 36 assessments with unresolved mapping and 12 with an unknown contract condition are intended results.
- Negative result: a simple alternative with the same data and conditions gave the same answers in all 30 pairs. Basis type: finite-test.
- A release cross-check by the kit authors shows separate rule conditions on tasks T02, T05, T08 and T12-T14. Basis type: authors-domain-assessment; a self-check by one assistant, with genres and hidden contexts not exhausted.

## Limits

- Hidden premises of arbitrary text are not detected; subject cases and three-book page processing remain not executed.
- A positive structural validation is not proof of correct understanding.
- Seven assessment records had `target.scope` inconsistent with the top-level `scope`, so no single verified applicability area was defined.
- Six open questions remain, such as automatic discovery of essential coordinates.
